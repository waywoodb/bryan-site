Fifty years in steel.
Now, clay.

Bryan Mackenzie spent a lifetime bending steel into spiral staircases. These days he fires porcelain in a wood-fired kiln he built on the factory floor — chasing colour, transparency, and the occasional glorious failure.

images/hero-portrait.jpg — Bryan with the oval platters (@bryanenz, 15 Dec 2025)
An idea in steel

In 1974, a fitter and turner in inner-north Melbourne wanted a staircase down to the cellar of his terrace house. He couldn't buy a spiral stair in Australia — so he designed one.

1974 — FitzroyThat staircase became Enzie. Friends wanted one, then their friends did, and soon a showroom opened on Gertrude Street with a factory squeezed out the back, occasionally overflowing into the laneways of Fitzroy. Bryan's modular steel design was so simple and so elegant that a renovator could assemble it in a day.

1980s — RecognitionThe awards followed: the Australian Design Award, the BHP Award for Consumer Products, a finalist's place for the Prince Philip Prize for Australian Design. Enzie stairs went on to climb through more than thirteen thousand homes across Australia and beyond — Asia, Europe, the Americas — and the business today carries on with his son William.

Now — North FitzroyBryan still lives where it all started, in a house he and his wife Marija — an artist — rebuilt for the long haul, with a chrome-yellow Enzie spiral at its heart. He chose that stair for its colour and its light, translucent quality. Which, as it turns out, is exactly what he's chasing now — just in a different material.

Celadon

The green that potters have chased for a thousand years. Bryan's celadons pool soft and deep in the bellies of hand-built bowls — sage through jade to olive — each one glazed against a row of small test chips lined up on the bench like samples in a toolroom. Old habits from a life of precision engineering die hard.

No two fire the same. That's the point.

Colour & transparency

Chrome yellow, kiln orange, cream — jugs, cups and nesting bowls glazed in the same bold, light-holding colours Bryan loved enough to put a bright yellow spiral staircase in the middle of his own home. These are pieces for the table, made to be used daily and looked through as much as at.

The table, assembled

Cornflower blue plates, bottle-green vessels, grey and sage bowls that sit together the way a good room does — nothing matching, everything belonging. Bryan builds these settings piece by piece, and the oval wall platters from the same family hang as quiet colour fields in their own right.

A kiln in a stair factory

The firing happens where the steel does. On the floor of the Enzie factory, between the racks of tube and the CNC machines, Bryan stacked the bricks for a wood-fired jet kiln by hand — lots of trial, plenty of error, and a story highlight honestly titled Will it hold?

Fifty years ago he built the thing he couldn't buy. He still does.
